# The Daily Cost of Revolution

**Bread, flour and sugar in American newspapers, 1770–1810** · Major Studio, Project 2 (PGDV 5200) · Sara Maria Vergara

How did the cost of everyday goods change between the Revolution and the early Republic? Every week, merchants' newspapers printed "price currents" (lists of what flour, sugar, coffee or rum sold for in port), and bakers' notices printed how much bread a coin would buy. This project reads those printed prices (about 3,100 after removing reprints) from Chronicling America newspapers (Library of Congress), through the Smithsonian's Revolution Crossroads datasets.
