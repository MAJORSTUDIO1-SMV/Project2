Confederation, 1784–1789

After a rush of British imports, the young country falls into a depression. Hard money is scarce, debts are hard to pay, and Britain closes its West Indian islands to American ships, a key market for flour and fish. Several states print paper money. Farmers' anger over debts and taxes leads to Shays' Rebellion in Massachusetts (1786–87). The Constitution is written in 1787 and the new government starts in 1789.

Assize of Bread

Same coin, bigger loaf

In New York the weight of a loaf was set by law from the price of flour. In 1779, under British occupation, 21 coppers bought two pounds. In 1787, six pence bought 2 lb. 5 oz.: a pound of bread cost about a third of what it had in the war.

Sources: New-York Gazette; and the Weekly Mercury, 1 Feb. 1779 · New-York Packet, 30 Oct. 1787. ≈ provisional conversion.

To the Reader

These pages show only what was printed and survives. The prices are wholesale prices printed in port for merchants, not what a family paid at the shop; about half were printed in pounds and shillings and converted to dollars (marked ≈). Each list is counted once, however often it was reprinted. The years and months for which no list survives are shown in the Explore section.
